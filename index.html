Strength
🏋️
Trap Bar Deadlift
aka Hex Bar Deadlift
Beginner-Friendly▼
Muscles Worked
Glutes, hamstrings, quads, lower back
How to Do It
Stand inside a hexagonal (diamond-shaped) bar with handles on your sides. Feet hip-width apart. Hinge at the hips and bend your knees, grip the handles, then drive through your heels to stand tall. Lower back down with control. Much easier to learn than a traditional deadlift — the weight stays centered on your body.
💡 Tip
If your gym doesn't have a trap bar, a regular dumbbell deadlift works fine to start.
🦵
Bulgarian Split Squat
aka Rear-Foot Elevated Split Squat
Moderate▼
Muscles Worked
Quads, glutes, single-leg stability
How to Do It
Stand about 2 feet in front of a bench. Place one foot behind you on the bench (laces down). Lower your back knee toward the floor by bending your front knee — like a lunge, but your rear foot is elevated. Keep your torso upright. Drive through your front heel to stand back up. Do all reps on one leg, then switch.
💡 Tip
This will feel awkward for the first few sessions. Use bodyweight only until balance is solid.
🏆
Goblet Squat
Beginner-Friendly▼
Muscles Worked
Quads, glutes, core, mobility
How to Do It
Hold a single dumbbell or kettlebell vertically at your chest with both hands cupped around the top (like holding a goblet). Feet shoulder-width apart, toes slightly out. Squat down keeping the weight at your chest, elbows tracking inside your knees. Stand back up.
💡 Tip
Great for building squat depth and mobility. A perfect starting exercise for your first few weeks.
⬇️
Romanian Deadlift (RDL)
aka Straight-Leg Deadlift
Beginner-Friendly▼
Muscles Worked
Hamstrings, glutes, lower back
How to Do It
Hold a barbell or dumbbells in front of your thighs, standing tall. With a slight bend in your knees (keep that bend constant), hinge forward at the hips — push your hips back while lowering the weight along your legs. You'll feel a deep stretch in your hamstrings. When you feel that stretch, drive your hips forward to stand tall again. The knees barely move — this is a hip hinge, not a squat.
💡 Tip
Think 'push hips back' rather than 'lower the weight.' Keep the bar close to your legs the whole time.
🦅
Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift
aka 1-Leg RDL
Moderate▼
Muscles Worked
Hamstrings, glutes, balance
How to Do It
Same movement as the RDL, but done on one leg. Hold a dumbbell in the opposite hand of your standing leg. Hinge at the hip, letting your non-standing leg float back behind you for balance as you lower the weight. Stand back up. Excellent for identifying strength imbalances between legs.
💡 Tip
Go light — balance is the entire point here, not load. It's much harder than it looks.
⬆️
Step-Ups (Explosive)
Beginner-Friendly▼
Muscles Worked
Quads, glutes, single-leg power
How to Do It
Stand in front of a sturdy box or bench (12–18 inches high). Step one foot up onto it, then drive explosively through that heel to bring your whole body up. Tap the opposite foot lightly on the box, then step back down. The key word is explosive — you're not just stepping up, you're powering up. Hold dumbbells at your sides for added resistance.
💡 Tip
The explosive push is what trains the fast-twitch muscle you need for dunking. Don't let this become a slow grinding movement.
🔝
Front Squat
Moderate▼
Muscles Worked
Quads, upper back, core
How to Do It
A barbell squat where the bar sits on the front of your shoulders (at your collarbone) instead of your back. Arms cross over the bar or you use a clean grip with elbows high. Requires a more upright torso and more quad strength than a back squat.
💡 Tip
If wrist or shoulder mobility is an issue, substitute a goblet squat — same muscle emphasis, zero equipment awkwardness.
⚡
Power Clean / Hang Clean
Advanced▼
Muscles Worked
Full body — explosive hip extension
How to Do It
Hold dumbbells at your thighs. Bend knees slightly and hinge forward a bit. Then explosively extend your hips and shrug — use that momentum to catch the dumbbells at shoulder height with elbows forward. It's a fast, athletic movement. The full barbell power clean is more technical.
💡 Tip
Skip this one if it feels confusing — trap bar deadlifts and jump squats cover the same athletic qualities more safely.
Plyometrics
📦
Box Jump
Beginner-Friendly▼
Muscles Worked
Explosive leg power, landing mechanics
How to Do It
Stand in front of a sturdy box (18–24 inches to start). Swing your arms back, bend your knees slightly, then explode upward and forward — landing softly on top of the box with both feet in a slight squat. Important: STEP back down one foot at a time. Do not jump back down.
💡 Tip
Land quietly. If you're landing loud, you're not absorbing force properly — lower the box until landings are controlled.
💥
Depth Jump
aka Drop Jump
Moderate▼
Muscles Worked
Reactive leg power — the most important exercise in this program
How to Do It
Step (don't jump) off a box 18–24 inches high. The moment both feet hit the ground, immediately explode upward as fast as possible — minimize your time on the ground. Think of the ground like a hot surface you want off immediately. This trains your tendons and nervous system to store and release energy like a spring.
💡 Tip
Low volume is everything. 4 sets of 5 reps with full recovery between sets. If you're doing 20 reps, you've turned it into cardio.
➡️
Broad Jump
aka Standing Long Jump
Beginner-Friendly▼
Muscles Worked
Horizontal explosive power
How to Do It
Stand with feet hip-width apart. Swing arms back, bend knees, then explode forward and out as far as possible — landing on both feet. Try to land softly and stick the landing in balance. Rest fully and repeat.
💡 Tip
Mark where you land each session. Tracking distance over weeks is one of the best ways to see real power gains.
🚀
Jump Squat
aka Squat Jump
Beginner-Friendly▼
Muscles Worked
Quads, glutes — bridging strength and power
How to Do It
Stand with feet shoulder-width apart. Lower into a quarter-squat (not a full squat), then explode upward as high as possible, leaving the ground completely. Land softly and reset. Bodyweight only to start. A light dumbbell (10–20 lb) at your chest can be added later.
💡 Tip
Keep these explosive. The moment they start feeling like conditioning, you're doing too many reps. Stop the set if jump height drops.
🏃
Continuous Broad Jumps
aka Bounding
Beginner-Friendly▼
Muscles Worked
Reactive power, coordination
How to Do It
Like a broad jump but in a chain — land and immediately explode into the next forward jump without pausing. Four to five jumps in a row equals one set. Focuses on the quick reactive transition between landing and jumping, similar to your legs during a running approach.
💡 Tip
Quality over distance on every rep. Don't let form fall apart chasing extra distance on the last jump.
Core & Mobility
🐛
Dead Bug
Beginner-Friendly▼
Muscles Worked
Deep core stability
How to Do It
Lie on your back, arms pointing straight up toward the ceiling, knees bent at 90 degrees in the air. Press your lower back firmly into the floor — keep it there the entire time. Slowly lower one arm overhead and the opposite leg toward the floor simultaneously, then return and switch sides.
💡 Tip
Go slow. This is not about speed. If your lower back arches off the floor, you've gone too far — shorten your range of motion.
💪
Pallof Press
aka Anti-Rotation Press
Beginner-Friendly▼
Muscles Worked
Core stability, rotational resistance
How to Do It
Attach a resistance band to a fixed point at chest height. Stand sideways to the anchor point, feet shoulder-width, and hold the band at your chest with both hands. Press your hands straight out in front of you, hold 2 seconds, then bring back to your chest. The band is pulling you sideways — your job is to resist and stay square. Switch sides each set.
💡 Tip
No bands? Swap this for a side plank. Same anti-rotation benefit, no equipment needed.
